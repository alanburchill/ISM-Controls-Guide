## Summary

Create at least two cloud-only break-glass emergency access accounts and assign them Global Administrator roles.[^2] Secure credentials with dual control by generating strong, randomly generated passwords of at least 32 characters that never expire, and store credential parts in separate secure locations.[^1] Configure MFA for break-glass accounts using a physical FIDO2 security key for MFA, and exclude at least one emergency account from Conditional Access policies to ensure access during service disruptions.[^1] Enable comprehensive monitoring and alerting for emergency account activity and conduct quarterly testing of break-glass procedures.[^1]

## Design Decision

> [!NOTE] Configure break-glass accounts with randomly generated passwords and enforce MFA using a physical FIDO2 key to provide strong, phishing-resistant emergency access.

## Prerequisites

* **Licensing:** Microsoft Entra Privileged Identity Management is included in Microsoft Entra ID P2 or EMS E5. A P2 license is required to use PIM. [^2]

* **Permissions/Roles:** Define at least two emergency break-glass cloud-only accounts with the Global Administrator role (not federated) for emergency access; assign initial Privileged Identity Management roles of Security Administrator and Privileged Role Administrator; enable MFA for privileged accounts. [^3][^2]

* **Dependencies:** Centralized identity management using Microsoft Entra ID; Privileged Identity Management (PIM) for just-in-time privileged access; monitoring and alerting for emergency access via Azure Monitor or Microsoft Sentinel; quarterly testing of break-glass procedures. [^5][^3][^1]

## Implementation Steps

### Setup emergency access (Break-glass) accounts

1. Create two cloud-only emergency access accounts with the Global Administrator role in Microsoft Entra ID. These accounts must not be tied to specific individuals and should be dedicated exclusively for emergency scenarios. Use descriptive names that clearly identify their purpose (e.g., EmergencyAccess01, BreakGlass02).[^1]

2. Secure credentials with dual control. Generate strong, randomly generated passwords of at least 32 characters and configure them to never expire. Split credentials into multiple parts and store them in separate secure physical locations, with retrieval procedures requiring multi-person approval.[^1]

3. Configure Conditional Access exclusions. Exclude at least one emergency access account from all Conditional Access policies and MFA requirements to guarantee access during service disruptions. Optionally, secure the second emergency account with FIDO2 security keys stored in secure locations to provide credential diversity.[^1]

4. Enable comprehensive monitoring and alerting. Configure a central monitoring solution to analyze sign-in and audit logs for emergency accounts. Create real-time alerts (email and SMS) triggered by emergency account authentication or configuration changes. Establish incident response procedures that require immediate security team notification and justification documentation for all emergency account usage.[^1]

5. Establish testing and maintenance procedures. Test emergency account access quarterly to verify functionality. Rotate credentials every 90 days or immediately after personnel changes affecting authorized users. Maintain written runbooks detailing the complete emergency access process for compliance and operational readiness.[^1]
